# Competitive Teardown: Period / Cycle-Tracking Apps

*Research date: August 2026. Every factual claim carries an inline source. Complaint quotes drawn from App Store / Google Play / Trustpilot coverage and tech reviews.*

---

## Part 1 — App-by-App Teardowns

### Flo
The category giant: ~380M downloads, ~70M MAU, ~5M premium payers, femtech's first unicorn ($200M+ Series C, July 2024) ([TechCrunch](https://www.techcrunch.com/2024/07/30/fertility-tracking-app-flo-health-raises-200m-at-a-1b-valuation)).
- **Standout:** Premium **Symptom Checker** flagging PCOS/endometriosis/fibroids as a pre-diagnostic tool; **"Ask Flo"** clinician-vetted chatbot; 70+ symptoms across period/ovulation/pregnancy/perimenopause; **Anonymous Mode**.
- **Pricing:** Free (increasingly gated); Premium **~$4.99–$10/mo or $39.99/yr**.
- **Best:** Breadth + scale — deepest medically-reviewed content + condition-screening.
- **Complaints:** ~1.6★ Trustpilot (~88% one-star); aggressive paywalling, billing/cancellation horror stories, UI bloat, teen-overrun chats. **Apple Health sync was removed** — a real gap.
- **Privacy:** The category's defining liability (Part 3).
- **Partner:** **Flo for Partners** (Oct 2023) — share predictions/pregnancy via a dedicated tab; basic free.

### Clue (BioWink, Berlin)
- **Standout:** The **science-first** tracker; named top free menstrual app in *Obstetrics & Gynecology*. Clean, gender-neutral, low-ad; 30+ free categories; **Conceive / Pregnancy / Perimenopause** modes.
- **Pricing:** Free tier permanent; **Clue Plus $9.99/mo or $39.99/yr**.
- **Best:** Most science-forward; top pick for privacy-conscious users.
- **Complaints:** Paywall creep; the 2022–23 relaunch stripped features from ~11M users (lost **birth-control reminders**); conservative algorithm; "calendar feels like a medical chart."
- **Partner:** **Clue Connect** — **one** partner, **one-directional**, not in Pregnancy Mode.
- **Ecosystem:** Two-way Apple Health sync; iOS 17+ widget. **No community, no AI chatbot.**

### Stardust (astrology + cycle)
- **Standout:** Fuses clinical tracking with astrology/lunar cycles — moon+cycle phases together, a mystical "witch type," daily horoscopes, 150+ symptom "Cyclepedia."
- **Pricing:** Free; Premium **$2.99/wk, $3.99/mo, $24.99/yr**.
- **Best:** Aesthetic/emotional engagement (~4.6★, 28K+ reviews).
- **Complaints:** "Glitchy and laggy," **can't adjust ovulation to actual timing**, refund refusals.
- **Privacy:** The category's marketing cautionary tale (Part 3).
- **Partner:** Add friends/partners, sync via a social "connect" tab.

### Natural Cycles (FDA-cleared contraception)
- **How:** FDA-cleared `NC°` algorithm reads post-ovulation BBT rise (~0.3°C) → **Red (fertile) / Green (not)** days. Needs waking temperature to two decimals.
- **Standout:** Birth Control / Plan / Follow Pregnancy / postpartum / perimenopause modes + **Partner View**. Overnight temp via **NC° Band, Oura, Apple Watch, Garmin**.
- **Pricing:** Subscription-only; monthly **rose to $21.99** (June 2025); ~$149.99/yr + hardware.
- **Best:** The **only FDA-cleared, hormone-free digital birth control** — **98% perfect / 93% typical use** (De Novo, Aug 2018).
- **Complaints:** Accuracy/unintended-pregnancy fears (a Stockholm hospital reported 37 abortions after use; UK ASA banned an ad as misleading); heavy **daily-measurement burden**.

### Apple Health / iOS Cycle Tracking (built-in)
- **Standout:** Health logging + Watch app; **retrospective ovulation estimates** from Series 8+ wrist temperature; **cycle-deviation notifications**; Pregnancy/Lactation/Contraceptive modes; 12-mo PDF export.
- **Pricing:** **Free / built into iOS** (Watch hardware for temp features).
- **Best:** Frictionless, private, no-cost logging tied to Apple Watch; underpins the 50K+ Apple Women's Health Study.
- **Complaints:** Deliberately shallow — no mood/headache-severity depth, once-per-day granularity, **no forward ovulation prediction** without basal temp, weak proactive prompting.
- **Partner:** Only generic Health Sharing; no bespoke partner mode. No community, chatbot, or education library.

### Ovia (Labcorp)
- **Standout:** Fertility / Pregnancy / Parenting apps; fertility forecasting; week-by-week pregnancy; rarely, **live nurse/midwife health coaches**.
- **Pricing:** Free; premium usually **employer/health-plan sponsored**.
- **Best:** The **B2B2C clinical-support model** (live coaches as an employer benefit).
- **Complaints:** Thin content; **intrusive data collection**; miscarriage edge-case failures. See employer-data controversy (Part 3).

### Glow / Eve by Glow
- **Standout:** AI ovulation calculator + clinical **BBT/OPK charts**; sells physical tests; **Eve** targets younger/non-TTC users with sexual-health logging + forums.
- **Pricing:** **$69.99/yr, $39.99/quarter, $99.99 lifetime**.
- **Complaints:** "Nearly all features locked behind a paywall," invasive popup ads, lost history. **Not a contraceptive.**
- **Privacy:** 2016 Consumer Reports breach + $250K CA AG settlement (Part 3).
- **Partner:** Two-way once partner installs; **all-or-nothing** (only a "Hidden from partner" flag).

### Cycles (couples-focused, Perigee AB)
- **Standout:** Flagship **Partner Connect** ("A Cycle Built for Two"); clear color-coded dial; **same-sex support**; Watch app. 4.7★ (~27K US).
- **Pricing:** Free + IAP; **no ads/data selling**; Premium gates Partner Connect.
- **Best:** Simplicity + pioneering couple involvement.
- **Complaints:** **Both partners must buy Premium**; everything manual; **no login/cloud backup**.
- **Privacy:** Swedish/GDPR; local-only, Face ID, discreet-icon disguise; no cloud = private but no backup.

### Spot On (Planned Parenthood)
- **Standout:** **Birth control is the core** — pill/patch/ring/shot/implant/IUD across 8 tailored flows with a **mistake-accommodation engine**; **Roo** chatbot; live PP educators.
- **Pricing:** **Fully free, no ads** (nonprofit); "data will never be for sale."
- **Best:** Best-in-class contraception *management* + trusted education, local-first.
- **Complaints:** Post-update data loss, stripped calendar.
- **Partner:** **None** — local-only architecture precludes sharing.

### Oura Ring (wearable)
- **Standout:** **Cycle Insights** — phase mapping + 5-day period window from temp/HRV/RHR; "twice as accurate for irregular cycles." Syncs temp into **Natural Cycles** (Oura itself not the contraceptive).
- **Pricing:** **Ring $349–$499 + $5.99/mo or $69.99/yr**.
- **Complaints:** Recurring sub atop pricey ring; ~2-mo learning; some abandoned it over post-Roe privacy fears.
- **Privacy:** Explicit pledge to oppose surveillance/prosecution requests; GDPR; not HIPAA-covered.

### Whoop (wearable)
- **Standout:** **Menstrual Cycle Coaching** — adjusts Strain/Sleep Coach by phase; 2025 added Cardiovascular amplitude + Clue integration.
- **Pricing:** **$199–$359/yr** (coaching is core, not paywalled).
- **Best:** Turns phase into **actionable training/recovery guidance**.
- **Complaints:** Not a medical device; no FDA contraception; weaker prediction; no explicit post-Roe law-enforcement pledge.

## Part 3 — PRIVACY LANDSCAPE (Post-Roe)

**Why it matters.** *Dobbs* (June 2022) turned reproductive data into potential criminal evidence. Most policies permit sharing "when asked by law enforcement or faced with a subpoena" ([EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/06/should-you-really-delete-your-period-tracking-app)). Precedent: **Nebraska (2022)** — Meta turned over Facebook DMs used in an abortion prosecution ([Nebraska Examiner](https://nebraskaexaminer.com/2022/08/10/facebook-data-used-to-prosecute-nebraska-mother-daughter-after-alleged-abortion/)). At least **210 pregnancy-related criminal cases** in the first post-Dobbs year.

**Flo — the enforcement cautionary tale.**
- **2021 FTC settlement:** shared health data with Facebook, Google, AppsFlyer, Flurry via SDKs despite privacy promises; agreed to remedies but **paid no monetary penalty** ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2021/01/developer-popular-womens-fertility-tracking-app-settles-ftc-allegations-it-misled-consumers-about)).
- **2025 Frasco v. Flo (CIPA):** Flurry, Flo ($8M), Google ($48M) settled (~$59.5M); **Meta went to trial and on Aug 1, 2025 a jury found it liable** under California's Invasion of Privacy Act — exposure estimated **up to ~$8B** ([Lawdragon](https://www.lawdragon.com/news-features/2025-08-25-big-tech-on-trial-jury-finds-meta-liable-for-misusing-women-health-data)).
- Response: **Anonymous Mode** (Sept 2022, Oblivious HTTP).

**Who credibly markets privacy.**
- **Clue (GDPR, EU):** "would not respond to any disclosure request… by US authorities"; EU storage; never sold.
- **Apple:** on-device + **E2E encryption with 2FA**; Advanced Data Protection. Caveat: E2E covers only the native Health app.
- **Natural Cycles:** **Go Anonymous / NC° Secure** cryptographically de-links identity.
- **Local-only (structurally safest):** **Euki** (Mozilla's only 10/10, decoy mode), **Drip** (open-source), **Periodical**.
- **Cycles / Spot On:** local-first, no data selling (Spot On leaks some web-flow search data to analytics — Mozilla 5/10).

**Stardust — marketing cautionary tale.** Hit #1 on privacy promises (June 2022), then reporting dismantled all three claims; sent phone numbers to Mixpanel; "E2E" was server-held keys. Mozilla 2/10 in 2026.

**Ovia — employer-data controversy.** 2019 WaPo investigation: paying employers access aggregated data (high-risk-pregnancy counts, return-to-work timing) with re-identification risk in small cohorts.

**Glow — breach history.** 2016 Consumer Reports: flaws let "a person with no hacking skills" read intimate data — driven by the **partner-linking feature** (invite anyone by email, no acknowledgement needed). $250K CA AG settlement (2020).

**Independent evals.** Mozilla flagged **18 of 25** products *Privacy Not Included* (Aug 2022); passing: Euki, Natural Cycles, all 5 wearables. Hands-on retest: Euki 10, Clue 8, Flo 7, Spot On 5, Stardust 2.

## Part 4 — PARTNER / COUPLE FEATURES (Today)

| App | Setup | Direction | Partner sees | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cycles** | Both install + invite | **One-way** (two-way for same-sex) | Phase, forecasts, fertile/period timing, "how to support" | **Both must buy Premium** |
| **Glow/Eve** | Invite by email | **Two-way** | Everything (all-or-nothing) + fertility hints | Free once linked |
| **Flo** | Partner tab, granular | One-way | Predictions / pregnancy progress | Basic free |
| **Clue** | Clue Connect | **One-way, one partner** | Cycle data (not in Pregnancy Mode) | Free |
| **Stardust** | Social tab | Two-way sync | Cycle sync among connections | Free tier |
| **Natural Cycles** | Partner View | One-way | Fertility status | In subscription |
| **Apple/Ovia/SpotOn/Oura/Whoop** | — | — | No dedicated cycle-partner mode | — |

**Good:** Cycles is the only partner-first app, framed as *support*, with same-sex parity. **Bad:** Cycles charges **both**; Glow is all-or-nothing (source of its breach); Clue is one-way and blocked in pregnancy. **Nobody offers bidirectional intimacy** — a shared space where the partner contributes rather than observes. Sharing today = surveillance-lite, not connection.

## Part 5 — THE GAPS (White Space)

**1. Genuine partner intimacy — the biggest opening.** Every partner feature today is one-way view-only or all-or-nothing. Nobody built a **bidirectional relational layer** — a shared space where a partner logs *their* observations, responds to prompts, acknowledges symptoms, plans together with per-field consent. Cycles proves demand but taxes it and keeps the partner a spectator.

**2. Thoughtful, non-nagging notifications.** Universal complaint is notification abuse (Eve's daily sub ads) or Apple's under-prompting. Nobody nailed **context-aware, low-frequency, genuinely useful nudges** for user *and* opted-in partner.

**3. Non-creepy AI.** Today it's either a medical-adjacent chatbot or buried prediction. White space: **on-device / privacy-preserving AI** giving warm, actionable guidance without shipping intimate data to a third-party model.

**4. Honest free tier + no double-charging.** Paywall creep and lost history are the single most common complaint across every app. Keep **core logging + history permanently free**; never charge both partners; never erase data on redesign.

**5. Privacy as architecture, not slogan.** Right now you must choose between *good privacy* (Euki, Apple, Spot On) and *good features/partner sharing* (Flo, Cycles, Glow). **Nobody offers both.** Local-first / E2E by default with verifiable claims + the partner/coaching features the privacy-safe apps lack.

**6. Reliable predictions + full ecosystem sync.** Flo dropped Apple Health sync; Clue has no community/AI; Cycles/Spot On have no cloud backup. Two-way Apple Health + Watch + wearable sync + cross-device backup + reliable widgets would beat each incumbent on its specific weakness.

**Bottom line:** The sharpest, least-served axis is **partner intimacy done respectfully** — bidirectional, consent-gated, privacy-preserving, warmly (not creepily) AI-assisted, on an honest free tier. Cycles proved couples want this; no one has built it without turning the partner into a spectator, the user into a surveillance subject, or the data into a product.
